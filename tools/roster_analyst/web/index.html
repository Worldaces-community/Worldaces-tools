Global

How a player's score is computed
  1. Stats: average of the sub-stats, weighted by the role's stat weights → 0-99. Balance exponent p turns it into a power mean (Σ weight × statp)1/p: at 1 it is the plain weighted average; below 1 low stats drag the score down more, which favours balanced profiles. Two equally weighted stats at 90 and 30 give 60 at p = 1 and ≈ 56 at p = 0.5, while 60 and 60 give 60 either way.
  2. Traits: each trait the player has adds its bonus × Trait weight, in points.
  3. Sign: the player's sign adds its bonus × Sign weight, in points. In the lineup it only counts when the sign effect is active (chemistry ≥ 3 with the 6 other starters).
  4. The total is rescaled so that the role's best possible player (99 in every weighted stat, the role's 4 best traits, its best sign) scores 99.

Example: stats 70, two traits rated 5 and 3, a sign rated 4 (both weights at 1), role ceiling 99 + 20 (4 best traits) + 6 (best sign) = 125 → (70 + 8 + 4) / 125 × 99 ≈ 65.

Setter connection: the setter activates no sign effect. Their connection bonus × Sign weight, rescaled the same way, is added to the lineup for each attacker connected with the setter (up to 5). It is not part of the setter's own score.

The weights multiply the bonuses, they are not shares of the score. With Trait weight 2 and Sign weight 0.5, a trait rated 5 adds 10 points and a sign rated 5 adds 2.5: at equal rating a sign is worth 4× less than one trait (2 / 0.5). What they really weigh also depends on your ratings, on the count (up to 4 traits, a single sign) and on activation (a sign counts only when active). Raising a weight also raises the role's ceiling, so the stats weigh a little less.
